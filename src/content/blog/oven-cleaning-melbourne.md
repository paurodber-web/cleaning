## How much does oven cleaning cost in Melbourne?

Maid At Home currently lists **inside-oven cleaning at $65** when selected as an optional extra with a Standard or Deep clean. The inside of the oven is included in the published core scope of Move In and End of Lease cleaning.

That is one provider's transparent price, not a universal Melbourne average. A specialist oven company may quote differently according to appliance size, configuration, buildup, racks, trays and whether separate components such as a cooktop, rangehood or barbecue are included.

Before comparing prices, check whether each quote covers the same work. See the live [oven cleaning service details](/services/oven-clean/) and [pricing page](/pricing/) for the current Maid At Home scope.

## What is usually included?

A typical inside-oven clean may address:

- accessible interior surfaces;
- removable oven racks and trays agreed in the scope;
- grease and cooked-on food residue;
- the accessible inside of the door and glass;
- an exterior wipe of the appliance;
- a final visual check after cleaning.

The exact checklist matters. These items should not be assumed:

- dismantling doors or removing glass panels;
- repairing seals, hinges, lights or controls;
- removing permanent staining, corrosion or enamel damage;
- cleaning behind a built-in appliance;
- cleaning a separate cooktop, rangehood or barbecue;
- handling a self-cleaning cycle;
- restoring trays or racks with existing rust or coating damage.

Ask for inclusions in writing. The phrase oven cleaning alone does not define every component.

## Oven cleaning versus kitchen cleaning

A routine kitchen clean normally focuses on reachable external surfaces, benches, sink, splashback and appliance exteriors. The oven interior is a separate, time-consuming task because baked-on grease needs product contact time and detailed work.

This is why a [Standard House Clean](/services/standard-clean/) does not automatically include the oven interior. A [Deep House Clean](/services/deep-clean/) gives more time to whole-home detail, but the oven is still selected separately unless the service checklist states otherwise.

For moving services, cleaning an empty oven is commonly part of the published core scope. Review the [end-of-lease cleaning cost guide](/blog/end-of-lease-cleaning-cost-melbourne/) to see how oven cleaning, carpets and optional extras affect the total.

## When is professional oven cleaning worthwhile?

A professional service can be practical when:

- grease and carbon have accumulated over a long period;
- the oven needs attention before a move or property inspection;
- you cannot safely bend, kneel or scrub for an extended time;
- you want to combine it with a broader house clean;
- you are unsure which household products are compatible with the finish;
- your available time is worth more than completing a slow DIY process.

It is not a repair service. If the oven has an electrical fault, damaged seal, broken glass, gas concern or loose component, stop using it and contact an appropriately qualified technician.

## A safe DIY oven cleaning approach

First read the appliance manual. Manufacturer instructions override generic advice, particularly for catalytic liners, pyrolytic ovens, aluminium parts and special coatings.

### 1. Switch off and let the oven cool

Never clean a hot oven. Turn controls off and allow the appliance to cool fully. If the manual calls for isolation from power, follow that instruction safely.

### 2. Ventilate and protect the area

Open suitable windows, keep children and pets away and protect nearby flooring. Wear the protective equipment stated on the cleaning product label.

### 3. Remove only approved components

Take out racks and trays if the manual permits. Do not force fittings, dismantle the door or remove glass unless the manufacturer gives clear instructions and you are competent to do so.

### 4. Remove loose debris

Wipe or vacuum loose crumbs with a suitable appliance-safe method. Avoid pushing debris into fans, vents, burners or electrical components.

### 5. Apply a compatible product

Use only a product suitable for the oven surfaces. Follow label dilution, contact time and ventilation requirements. Never mix cleaning chemicals, especially bleach, acids or ammonia-based products.

### 6. Wipe and rinse as directed

Use non-scratch tools where required. Remove residue thoroughly according to the product directions, because remaining cleaner can create odour or smoke when the appliance heats.

### 7. Dry, reassemble and inspect

Let parts dry, replace them correctly and check that no cloth, protector or loose debris remains. Follow the manual before heating the oven again.

## What not to do

Avoid these common mistakes:

- using a metal scraper on coated or glass surfaces without approval;
- spraying product into heating elements, fans, ignition points or vents;
- mixing products to make them stronger;
- assuming a self-cleaning cycle is suitable for every oven or every rack;
- lining the oven with foil if the manufacturer prohibits it;
- cleaning damaged glass or electrical components as if they were cosmetic issues;
- promising that old discolouration or enamel damage can be removed.

More force does not always produce a better result. It can turn residue into permanent surface damage.

## How long does oven cleaning take?

For planning, a maintained single oven may take roughly **one to two hours** of active work plus product contact and drying time. Heavy buildup, multiple racks, a double oven or delicate components can take longer.

This is a guide rather than a booking promise. Send clear photos when requesting a quote, especially if there is thick carbon, melted material or damage.

## How often should an oven be cleaned?

There is no universal schedule. Frequency depends on use and what is cooked. A household that roasts frequently will need more attention than one that rarely uses the oven.

A practical maintenance routine is:

- wipe fresh spills only after the oven has cooled and when the manual permits;
- check the base and door regularly for grease buildup;
- clean trays separately before residue becomes heavily carbonised;
- schedule a deeper clean when smoke, odour or visible buildup appears;
- include the oven in moving plans before the final inspection.

Smoke or unusual smells can also indicate a fault. If basic cleaning does not explain the problem, arrange technical assessment.

## Oven cleaning for end of lease

For a vacate clean, leave the oven accessible and empty. Remove personal liners, trays that do not belong to the property and loose kitchen items. Confirm whether the cooktop and rangehood are separate tasks in the quote.

Existing damage should be documented rather than scrubbed aggressively. Victorian renters should compare the final state with the entry condition report and account for fair wear and tear. ## Questions to ask a professional cleaner

Before booking, ask:

- Is the price for a single or double oven?
- Which racks and trays are included?
- Is the cooktop or rangehood included?
- Do you remove the door or glass panels?
- What condition or damage should I disclose?
- Do you need power, hot water or ventilation?
- How should I prepare the appliance?
- Is the price an extra or included in the selected moving service?
- How long should I avoid using the oven afterwards?

The written answers make it easier to compare scope, price and responsibility.
