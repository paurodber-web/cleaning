## Start with the result you need

Before comparing Melbourne cleaners, define the job. A weekly maintenance clean, a one-off whole-home reset, a move-out service and carpet steam cleaning require different time, equipment and checklists.

Write down:

- the suburb and property type;
- bedrooms and bathrooms;
- whether the home is occupied or empty;
- the condition and last professional clean;
- pets, parking, stairs and building access;
- priority rooms or problem areas;
- extras such as oven, fridge, windows, walls or carpet;
- the date and any genuine deadline.

This prevents a low quote based on an incomplete description from becoming an expensive or disappointing booking later.

## 1. Ask for a written scope

The scope should say what is included, what is excluded and which tasks cost extra. Terms such as regular, deep or bond clean are not universal definitions.

For example, ask whether the service includes:

- skirting boards and reachable high dusting;
- shower screens and bathroom buildup;
- inside the oven, fridge or cabinets;
- interior windows, tracks and blinds;
- wall washing or spot cleaning;
- balcony areas;
- carpet steam cleaning;
- rubbish removal or only emptying household bins.

Use the guide to [what a professional house clean includes](/blog/what-is-included-in-house-clean/) as a comparison list. If a task matters, it should appear in the booking rather than being assumed.

## 2. Compare the total price, not only the hourly rate

A low hourly figure does not reveal the total if there is a minimum booking, travel charge, equipment fee, weekend rate or additional time for extras. A fixed price is also only useful when the included condition and tasks are clear.

Ask:

- Is the quote fixed or hourly?
- Is GST included?
- Is there a minimum duration or room count?
- Are products and equipment included?
- Which extras can change the total?
- Does difficult parking or access add a fee?
- What is the cancellation or rescheduling policy?

## 3. Verify the business and contact details

Look for a consistent business name, working contact details, service area and clear website policies. If the provider states an ABN, insurance, licences, police checks or training credentials, ask how those claims apply to the person attending your home.

Do not rely on a logo or social-media follower count as proof. A professional provider should be willing to answer practical questions without pressuring you into immediate payment.

## 4. Ask how cleaners are selected and supported

Depending on the business model, the person attending may be an employee, contractor or independent operator. Ask:

- Who assigns the cleaner?
- What identity or background checks are used?
- What training or onboarding is provided?
- Who do I contact during the booking?
- Can the same person or team return for recurring visits?
- What happens if the assigned cleaner is unavailable?

The goal is not a particular employment model. It is clarity about responsibility, communication and continuity.

## 5. Discuss insurance without making assumptions

Ask whether the business or cleaner holds appropriate current insurance and what it covers. If the answer matters to your decision, request evidence and read the relevant limits rather than relying on a general statement.

Also ask how accidental damage is reported. Photograph valuable, delicate or already damaged surfaces before the first visit and disclose materials such as natural stone, unsealed timber, specialty coatings or fragile fittings.

## 6. Read reviews critically

A useful review describes the service, timing, communication and outcome. Look for patterns across recent reviews rather than relying on one perfect or angry comment.

Check:

- whether reviews appear across an independent platform;
- whether recent customers mention the service you need;
- how the company responds to criticism;
- whether complaints show a repeated issue;
- whether praise is specific or generic;
- whether review dates indicate ongoing activity.

Reviews help assess consistency, but they do not replace a written scope for your property.

## 7. Confirm products, equipment and surface limits

Ask who supplies the vacuum, mop, cloths and cleaning products. If you prefer your own products, confirm whether the cleaner will use them and who is responsible for suitability.

Disclose allergies, fragrance preferences and sensitive materials before the visit. Avoid demanding that cleaners mix products or use an unlabelled chemical. No responsible service should promise that every stain, mould mark, mineral deposit or damaged finish can be restored through ordinary cleaning.

Specialist work such as pest treatment, hazardous waste, high exterior windows, extensive mould remediation or repairs should be referred to an appropriately qualified provider.

## 8. Agree on safe property access

A good booking process should explain access clearly. Decide whether someone will be home, a key is handed over, a lockbox is used or building management provides entry.

Ask:

- How are keys or access codes recorded and protected?
- Will the cleaner call if access fails?
- Are parking and lift bookings my responsibility?
- How should pets be secured?
- When will I know the cleaner has finished?
- What happens if the home cannot be entered?

Never place access codes, alarm details or hidden-key instructions in a public review or social-media message.

## 9. Understand changes, cancellations and issues

Read the cancellation, rescheduling and access policy before paying. For hourly work, clarify who can approve extra time. For fixed-price work, clarify which changes in condition or scope may require a revised quote.

Ask how quickly a concern must be reported, what evidence is useful and what remedy may be available. Avoid assuming that a cleaning service guarantees a rental bond, a property sale, complete stain removal or a particular inspection result.

Take photos promptly if something is missed or damaged, and give the provider a reasonable opportunity to assess the issue.

## 10. Test communication before committing to recurring service

The quotation process is an early test. Strong signs include:

- questions about property size and condition;
- a written confirmation of date, price and scope;
- realistic limits rather than absolute promises;
- clear arrival or access instructions;
- a named contact channel for changes;
- transparent policies available before the service.

Warning signs include pressure to pay immediately, refusal to define the work, unclear business identity, requests for sensitive information through insecure channels or a price that changes whenever you ask what is included.

For an ongoing clean, start with a single visit if you want to assess fit. Discuss priorities afterwards and decide whether the frequency and scope match how the home is actually used.

## Prepare for the first visit

Once booked, clear loose objects from the main surfaces, secure valuables and pets, explain access and identify priority areas. Do not pre-clean the entire property, but make it possible for the cleaner to reach the surfaces you are paying to have cleaned.
